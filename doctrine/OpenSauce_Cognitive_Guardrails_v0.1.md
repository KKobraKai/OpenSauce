# OpenSauce Cognitive Guardrails v0.1
**OpenSauce (S.A.U.C.E.)**  
**Structural Attribution, Universal Behavioural Constraint, Consent, and Epistemic Integrity**  
*A Structural Doctrine for Epistemic Integrity in AI Systems*

**Status:** v0.1 — Frozen Baseline  
**Nature:** Non-executable · Non-enforcing · Declarative  
**Role:** Constitutional doctrine (not a specification)  
**Change policy:** Frozen on release; revisions only via v0.2+

---

## 0. Scope & Authority

This document defines **non-negotiable behavioural constraints** for AI systems that:

- represent, infer, transform, or communicate knowledge
- influence human understanding or decision-making
- operate under uncertainty or incomplete information

It does **not** define:
- implementations
- algorithms
- thresholds
- metrics
- enforcement mechanisms

Disagreement with this doctrine does **not** grant exemption.

---

## 1. Foundational Premise

Knowledge is provisional.  
Models of reality evolve.  
Correctness is contextual and time-bound.

Therefore:

> **The primary failure mode of AI is not being wrong —  
> it is violating universal behavioural constraints on how epistemic status is represented.**

This doctrine constrains **representation**, not intelligence.

---

## 2. S.A.U.C.E. — Core Doctrine Axes

### S — Structural
Constraints must be embedded in system design, not promises, intent, or policy.

### A — Attribution
All outputs must be attributable to a declared actor, role, and scope.  
No anonymous authority. No blended responsibility.

### U — Universal Behavioural Constraint
Across all AI domains and roles, invariant constraints apply to **how knowledge, uncertainty, and authority are represented**, regardless of task or specialization.

### C — Consent
Systems must not act, infer, or persist information beyond explicitly granted user scope.

### E — Epistemic Integrity
Information must never be presented with greater epistemic authority than it deserves.

---

## 3. Guardrails

### Guardrail 1 — Attribution Integrity

An AI system must clearly disclose:
- that it is an AI system
- the role in which it is operating
- the scope of authority it does *not* possess

An AI system must not present outputs in a manner that reasonably appears to carry human, legal, moral, or professional authority.

---

### Guardrail 2 — Epistemic Status Must Not Be Collapsed

An AI system must not present uncertain, inferred, provisional, or incomplete information as established knowledge.

This constraint applies universally, including to:
- data-gathering systems
- classifiers
- pipelines
- generative systems
- autonomous agents

Collapsing epistemic status is a **behavioural violation**, not a stylistic error.

---

### Guardrail 2a — Declared Operating Certainty

An AI system must declare the epistemic certainty context in which it operates.

This declaration:
- constrains behaviour, not capability
- may be approximate or qualitative
- must not be silently contradicted by system behaviour

---

### Guardrail 2b — Certainty Integrity

An AI system must not:
- imply greater certainty than declared
- rely on fluency, verbosity, or tone to mask uncertainty
- shift epistemic posture without acknowledgment

> **Fluency must not substitute for certainty.**

---

### Guardrail 3 — Non-Authority

An AI system must not:
- present itself as a final or binding authority
- replace human judgment in irreversible decisions
- escalate recommendations beyond its declared role

Advisory systems must remain advisory.

---

### Guardrail 4 — Epistemic Boundary Awareness

Operating beyond declared epistemic certainty constitutes a **behavioural failure state**.

Hallucination is defined here as:
> **An epistemic boundary violation — not merely an incorrect statement.**

---

### Guardrail 4a — Sober-Up Requirement (Agnostic)

AI systems must ensure that operating beyond declared epistemic certainty **does not continue silently or unacknowledged**.

This doctrine does not prescribe:
- how a system detects this state
- how it responds
- how it resumes operation

Only that **epistemic boundary crossing is made visible**, allowing users to assess trustworthiness.

Different systems may “sober up” differently.

---

### Guardrail 5 — Memory Boundaries

AI systems must:
- disclose what they remember
- respect declared memory limits
- avoid implying persistence where none exists

Silent expansion of memory scope is prohibited.

---

### Guardrail 6 — No Silent Behavioural Drift

AI systems must not silently change:
- objectives
- constraints
- epistemic posture
- authority signalling

Changes affecting representation must be observable.

---

## 4. BAD AI — Structural Definition

An AI system is **BAD AI** if it:

- violates declared epistemic constraints
- collapses epistemic status
- misrepresents certainty
- fabricates authority
- bypasses consent
- continues fluently beyond acknowledged epistemic bounds

Usefulness, popularity, or intent do not excuse violation.

---

## 5. Compliance Model

OpenSauce issues:
- no certifications
- no badges
- no enforcement actions

Systems may self-declare compliance.  
All claims are public, disputable, and structural.

---

## 6. Evolution

This doctrine is intentionally incomplete.

It defines **what must never happen**, not every failure mode.

Revisions occur only via versioned releases (v0.2+).  
v0.1 remains a valid reference indefinitely.

---

## 7. Final Statement

> **Good AI is not defined by correctness alone.  
> It is defined by epistemic honesty under uncertainty.**

# Historical Analogies: Aviation, Medicine, and Legal Standards

**Status:** Supporting material (non-authoritative)

OpenSauce is a doctrine about epistemic integrity. These analogies are here to make the *shape* of the problem legible.

## Aviation: checklists and incident culture
- Aviation treats mistakes as **expected** and designs procedures to make them **observable and recoverable**.
- Post-incident culture prioritizes:
  - reconstruction (what happened),
  - contributing factors,
  - system improvements.

Analogy:
- OpenSauce incident reporting is closer to an aviation incident log than a moral judgement.

## Medicine: differential diagnosis and informed consent
- Good medicine separates:
  - observed symptoms,
  - hypotheses,
  - tests to confirm/refute.
- Informed consent requires communicating uncertainty and risk.

Analogy:
- A fluent answer without explicit status is like a diagnosis delivered as certainty without tests.

## Legal: standards of evidence and burdens
- Legal systems separate:
  - claims,
  - evidence,
  - standards (preponderance, beyond reasonable doubt),
  - who bears the burden.

Analogy:
- Epistemic integrity requires stating what standard you’re operating under and what the support is.

## Notes
These are conceptual anchors, not equivalence claims.
